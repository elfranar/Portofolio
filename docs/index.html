Featured Project

CO₂ to Methanol Reactor Project

01. Process Overview

Fig. 1 — AutoCAD Process Flow Diagram (PFD) for CO₂ Hydrogenation

  • Feed Compression: Inlet feed consists of pure CO₂ (100 kg/hr) and H₂ (13.8 kg/hr) mixed at a 3:1 stoichiometric ratio, compressed to a system pressure of 75 bar.
  • Thermal Integration: The cold reactant gas is preheated to the reaction temp of 220 °C through a feed-effluent heat exchanger utilizing the high-temperature product stream.
  • Reaction System: Catalytic conversion takes place in a multi-tubular reactor. A dual-bed configuration with interstage cooling keeps temperatures at ≤ 210 °C to sustain equilibrium conversion.
  • Separation & Recycle: Crude liquid methanol (~64 wt% purity) is separated in a high-pressure flash separator. Unreacted gases are redirected through a recycle loop at a recycle ratio of R = 4.2.

02. Engineering Analysis

  • Design GHSV: 5,000 h⁻¹

  • Heat Exchange Area: 120.0 m²
Conversion Assumptions
  • Single-Pass Conversion: ~25% CO₂
  • Overall Carbon Conversion: >95%
  • Operating T Range: 210 – 260 °C
  • Reaction Exotherm (ΔH): −49.5 kJ/mol
  • Recycle Ratio (R): 4.2

03. Reactor Design Insight

Fig. 2 — AutoCAD Reactor Cross-Section Drawing

Operating Pressure75 bar
Temperature ProfileInlet: 220 °C | Hotspot: max 260 °C
Catalyst SpecsCu/ZnO/Al₂O₃ | 3 mm × 3 mm cylindrical pellets
Tube Sizing680 tubes | Outer Diameter: 38 mm | Length: 3.5 m
Total Catalyst Load1.2 Metric Tons
Design Selection Rationale: A multi-tubular configuration was selected to maximize the heat-transfer surface area, allowing efficient removal of the reaction heat (ΔH = −49.5 kJ/mol) via boiling water on the shell side. A dual-bed system with interstage cooling is utilized to prevent catalyst sintering (which occurs above 280 °C) and bypass thermodynamic equilibrium limitations to increase conversion.

04. Material & Safety Consideration

Material Selection
  • SS316L (Reactor Shell & Tubes): Selected for high-pressure containment (75 bar) because it offers superior resistance to hydrogen embrittlement and carbonic acid corrosion from wet CO₂.
  • Carbon Steel A516 Gr.70 (Jacket): Selected for the outer cooling jacket shell to reduce material costs while safely containing low-pressure boiling water.
  • SS316 clad Carbon Steel (Tube Sheets): Combines mechanical structural strength with corrosion resistance at tube joints.
Leak Prevention & Safety
  • Double-Welded Tube Sheets: Prevents bypass leaks between high-pressure process gas inside the tubes and shell-side coolant.
  • Thermal Expansion Bellows: Integrated on the shell to absorb stress from temperature cycles (210–260 °C) and prevent stress-corrosion cracking.
  • Pressure Relief Valves (PRVs): Installed on the reactor inlet and separator gas output to vent pressure in case of runaway reactions or blockages.

07. Reflection & Improvement

What I Learned

Gained comprehensive experience designing an industrial-scale reactor system end-to-end. I learned how to translate theoretical reaction kinetics and mass balances into mechanical AutoCAD equipment designs, developing a deep understanding of thermal management limitations in highly exothermic catalytic operations.

Limitations

The project's design and operating parameters are strictly modeled under ideal, steady-state simulations using literature kinetic rate constants. Real-world catalyst decay, localized hot-spots, and flow distribution abnormalities inside the tubes can only be verified with physical laboratory scale validation.

Next Steps

My next step is to further study and simulate the transient process flows in **ASPEN HYSYS** to analyze startup behavior and feed variations. Furthermore, I aim to conduct **experimental validation** using micro-reactor tests once laboratory-scale testing begins.
