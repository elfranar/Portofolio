01 Process Flow Diagram

Fig. 1 — Drafted PFD: CO₂ Hydrogenation to Methanol · Dual-Bed Configuration · P = 75 bar

  • Feed: CO₂ (100 kg/hr) + H₂ (13.8 kg/hr) at molar ratio H₂:CO₂ = 3:1, compressed to 75 bar
  • Inlet gas preheated to 220 °C via feed-effluent heat exchanger (HEX area = 120 m²)
  • Dual-bed configuration with interstage cooling at ≤ 210 °C — sustains equilibrium-limited conversion beyond single-pass limits
  • HP flash separator recovers crude methanol (~64 wt%); unreacted gases recycled at recycle ratio R = 4.2
  • Overall carbon utilization: > 95% achieved through closed recycle loop integration

03 Reactor Design

Fig. 2 — AutoCAD Cross-Section: Dual-Bed Tubular Reactor Vessel

Reactor TypeDual-Bed Fixed Bed Tubular (Shell-and-Tube)
Operating T210–250 °C · interstage cooling ≤ 210 °C
Operating P75 bar
CatalystCu/ZnO/Al₂O₃ · pellets 3 mm × 3 mm
No. of Tubes680 tubes · OD 38 mm · L 3.5 m
Shell ID1,200 mm · wall thickness 45 mm
Catalyst load1.2 T total charge
Shell materialSS316L (75 bar ASME service)
Design Rationale: Multi-tubular shell-and-tube configuration selected for maximized heat-transfer surface area to remove continuous exothermic load (ΔH = −49.5 kJ/mol). Boiling-water shell side generates LP process steam. Dual-bed interstage cooling prevents catalyst sintering (Tsinter > 280 °C) and pushes conversion beyond single-pass thermodynamic limits.

06 Project Reflection
What I Learned

Designed and evaluated a full CCU reactor system end-to-end — from reaction stoichiometry and steady-state mass balances through AutoCAD PFD drafting and high-pressure SOP authoring. Bridged kinetic modeling with physical equipment sizing; developed deep understanding of heat-transfer constraints in exothermic catalytic systems.

Limitations

All process parameters were derived from steady-state computational models and peer-reviewed kinetic correlations. Physical bench-scale validation — including real catalyst activity profiling, hotspot temperature measurement, and gas flow distribution — remains to be conducted prior to pilot-scale consideration.

Next Steps
  • Dynamic simulation in ASPEN HYSYS for transient start-up & feed fluctuations
  • Micro-reactor bench test to calibrate modeled vs. experimental kinetic constants
  • CFD analysis of radial temperature profiles & tube-side velocity distribution
